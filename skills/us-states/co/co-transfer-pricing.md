# Colorado transfer pricing & related-party rules, tax year 2026

> **Source-cited draft.** Revised 2026-10-28 against the Colorado Revised Statutes 2026 and the Corporate Income Tax Guide (March 2026). v0.1 cited the related-party adjustment authority as "C.R.S. §39-22-503" and tax-haven combined reporting as "C.R.S. §39-22-5303"; in the 2026 statutes §39-22-503 is "Taxation of real estate investment trusts" and §39-22-5303 is "Contributions credited to Feeding Colorado fund", so both citations have been corrected to §39-22-304. The statute text was unreachable on earlier runs; the substance below was read from the archived 2026 statutes. No accountant has reviewed this draft.

## Who this applies to

- C corporations that are owned or controlled, directly or indirectly, by the same interests: the executive director may, to avoid abuse, on a fair and impartial basis, distribute or allocate gross income and deductions between or among them to clearly reflect income ([C.R.S. §39-22-304(6)](https://olls.info/crs/crs2026-title-39.pdf)).
- Affiliated C corporations in combined reports, including members organized in listed (low- or no-tax) jurisdictions: C.R.S. §39-22-304(8) and (8.5) (Colorado Revised Statutes 2026).
- C corporations claiming the federal foreign-derived deduction eligible income (FDII) deduction for income tax years commencing on or after 1 January 2026: HB 25B-1002 adds the claimed federal deduction back to federal taxable income ([HB 25B-1002](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb25b-1002)).
- Entities apportioning income across states: receipts-factor apportionment under C.R.S. §39-22-303.6 (referenced on the DR 0106 instructions).

## Rates, bands and thresholds

| Mechanism | Effect | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Corporate rate | 4.4% flat (2025; 2024 was 4.25%) | [Corporate Income Tax Guide, Part 3](https://tax.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Corporate_Income_Tax_Guide_Mar_2026_.pdf) |
| Related-party allocation | Gross income and deductions may be distributed or allocated among C corporations owned or controlled by the same interests, to clearly reflect income and avoid abuse | C.R.S. §39-22-304(6) |
| Combined reporting | A combined return must include each affiliate satisfying at least three of the six tests of unity for the current and two preceding tax years | Corporate Income Tax Guide, Part 2; C.R.S. §39-22-304 |
| Listed jurisdictions | A C corporation organized in a listed foreign jurisdiction is presumed incorporated for tax avoidance unless it proves otherwise under the economic-substance doctrine | C.R.S. §39-22-304(8); [HB 25B-1002](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb25b-1002) |
| Water's-edge election | Binding for the election year and each of the nine following income tax years | C.R.S. §39-22-304(8.5) |
| FDII addback (from 1 January 2026) | A C corporation adds back the federal deduction claimed for foreign-derived deduction eligible income | HB 25B-1002 |
| Apportionment | Receipts-factor apportionment; direct sourcing election for some items | C.R.S. §39-22-303.6, as referenced on the DR 0106 instructions |

The six tests of unity (Corporate Income Tax Guide, Part 2), of which at least three must be met for the current and two preceding years: (1) gross receipts -- 50% or more of gross operating receipts from, or cost of goods purchased from, another affiliate; (2) services -- 50% or more of five or more listed services received from affiliates without an arm's-length charge; (3) long-term debt -- 20% of long-term debt owed to or guaranteed by an affiliate; (4) intangible property -- substantial use of another affiliate's patents, trademarks, copyrights or other proprietary materials; (5) board of directors -- 50% or more shared board members; (6) a sixth test as published in the guide.

## The taxable base

- Colorado corporate taxable income starts from federal taxable income with the additions and subtractions in the Corporate Income Tax Guide, Parts 4 and 5 (for example, non-Colorado state and local bond interest is added; dividends between includible members of a combined report are excluded).
- Dividends a C corporation includible in a combined report receives from another includible member are excluded from taxable income (Corporate Income Tax Guide, Part 3).
- For income tax years beginning before 1 January 2027, neither the taxpayer nor the executive director shall include in a combined report a C corporation with 80% or more of its property and payroll outside the United States, subject to the listed-jurisdiction exception (C.R.S. §39-22-304(8)).
- No activity outside Colorado → 100% of income sourced to Colorado; ordinary income of a partnership is generally sourced using receipts-factor apportionment even without a full election (DR 0106 instructions).

## Deadlines and filing

- Adjustments follow the return and assessment cycle: DR 0112 is due the fifteenth day of the fifth month after year close (May 15 for calendar-year filers), with an automatic six-month extension to November 15 and no extension of payment ([DR 0112 (2025) instructions](https://tax.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/documents/DR0112_2025.pdf)); DR 0106 is due April 15 with extension to October 15 (DR 0106 instructions).
- A combined group's water's-edge election must be made on a timely filed, original return (C.R.S. §39-22-304(8.5)).
- Assessment and refund periods follow C.R.S. article 21; confirm the current limitations before planning.

## Penalties and interest

- Underpaid tax from adjustments accrues interest at the rate fixed under C.R.S. §39-21-110.5 (Colorado Revised Statutes 2026; v0.1 cited §39-21-118, which in the 2026 statutes is "Criminal penalties").
- Delinquent payment penalty: the greater of $5 or 5% of the additional tax due for the first month of delinquency plus 0.5% for each additional month, up to 12%, avoided if 90% of the tax is paid by the original due date (DR 0106 instructions).

## Cross-border points

- Combined reporting with listed jurisdictions is Colorado's principal cross-border anti-avoidance regime; confirm the current listed-jurisdiction list and the economic-substance rebuttal before advising (C.R.S. §39-22-304(8); HB 25B-1002 changed the presumption so the C corporation no longer must rebut it).
- Federal international provisions (GILTI/FDII) conformity: HB 25B-1002 adds the FDII deduction back for Colorado C corporations for income tax years commencing on or after 1 January 2026, and modifies the subtraction for dividends from foreign subsidiaries.
- Public Law 86-272 applies to a net-income tax on protected solicitation (federal -- see `co-cross-border`).
- Foreign-source income exclusions and the export-trading-company rules are addressed in the Corporate Income Tax Guide, Part 7 (Foreign Source Income Exclusion).

## Worked examples

A Colorado corporation pays $1,000,000 in royalties to a wholly owned affiliate in a low-tax country (hypothetical facts). The Department reallocizes the deduction to an arm's-length $600,000; the $400,000 disallowed amount is added back and taxed at the confirmed 4.4% rate:

```oa-check
name: royalty addback tax effect
inputs:
  paid: 1000000
  arm_length: 600000
  rate: 0.044
steps:
  - disallowed: paid - arm_length
  - tax: disallowed * rate
expected:
  disallowed: 400000.00
  tax: 17600.00
```

A multistate licensor earns $8,000,000 of royalty income, of which receipts sourced to Colorado are $2,000,000 (hypothetical facts; receipts factor per C.R.S. §39-22-303.6):

```oa-check
name: receipts-factor royalty apportionment
inputs:
  co_receipts: 2000000
  total_receipts: 8000000
  rate: 0.044
steps:
  - factor: co_receipts / total_receipts
  - apportioned: total_receipts * factor
  - tax: apportioned * rate
expected:
  factor: 0.25
  apportioned: 2000000.00
  tax: 88000.00
```

## Ask the client first

- What intercompany interest, rent, royalty or service fees exist with related parties, domestic or foreign?
- Are any affiliates organized in low- or no-tax jurisdictions -- is combined reporting or a listed-jurisdiction presumption in play?
- Does the group meet at least three of the six tests of unity (gross receipts, services, long-term debt, intangibles, board overlap)?
- Has the C corporation claimed the federal FDII deduction -- remember the Colorado addback from 1 January 2026?
- Is transfer-pricing documentation contemporaneous and defensible under IRC §482 and Colorado's allocation authority?
