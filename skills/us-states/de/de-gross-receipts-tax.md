# Delaware gross receipts tax (GRT), 2026

> **Source-cited draft.** Built by Michael Cutajar and the OpenAccountants team from Title 30, Chapters 21-29 of the Delaware Code and the Delaware Division of Revenue's gross receipts tax pages, read on 2026-10-28. No accountant has reviewed it. Do not advise a client from it without review by a qualified Delaware professional.

## Who this applies to

**Every person or entity engaged in business in Delaware.** No person shall engage in or carry on any trade or business for which a license is required without first obtaining a license from the Department of Finance and paying the prescribed fee or tax ([30 Del. C. § 2101(a)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title30/c021/index.html)). This includes sole proprietors, corporations, partnerships, LLCs, and independent contractors providing services in Delaware. The GRT is a tax on the total gross revenues of a business, regardless of their source, levied on the seller of goods or services rather than on the consumer ([Division GRT FAQs](https://revenue.delaware.gov/gross-receipts-tax/)).

**Self-employed and sole proprietors.** This skill is written for the self-employed: registration, category selection, exclusions, filing frequency and payment. A business with employees has the same GRT obligations, but payroll taxes are outside this skill.

**Not covered:** Delaware corporate income tax, personal income tax, franchise tax (see `de-franchise-tax-and-llc`), or the separate license regimes for insurance premiums and banks.

## Rates, bands and thresholds

Delaware has no general sales tax; the GRT is the state's primary transaction-level tax on the seller. Rates currently range from .0945% to 1.9914%, or a variable rate on petroleum products which may be as high as 2.4218%, depending on the business activity ([Division GRT FAQs](https://revenue.delaware.gov/gross-receipts-tax/)). The rates and exclusions below are the most common categories, from the Division's license and rate chart and the governing chapters of Title 30.

| Business category | Tax rate | Exclusion (per month, unless noted) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retailers, general | 0.7468% | $100,000 | [30 Del. C. § 2902(b)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title30/c029/index.html) |
| Wholesalers | 0.3983% | $100,000 | [30 Del. C. § 2903(c)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title30/c029/index.html) |
| Manufacturers | 0.126% | $1,250,000 | [30 Del. C. § 2702(b)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title30/c027/index.html) |
| General services (professional, occupational) | 0.3983% | $100,000 | [30 Del. C. § 2301(d)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title30/c023/index.html) |
| Contractors | 0.6472% | $100,000 | [30 Del. C. § 2502(c)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title30/c025/index.html) |
| Restaurant retailers | 0.6472% | $100,000 | [30 Del. C. § 2904(c)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title30/c029/index.html) |
| Food processors | 0.1991% | $100,000 | [Division rate chart](https://revenuefiles.delaware.gov/docs/gr_rates.pdf) |
| Commercial feed dealers | 0.0996% | $100,000 | [Division rate chart](https://revenuefiles.delaware.gov/docs/gr_rates.pdf) |
| Commercial lessors | 0.3983% | $100,000 | [Division rate chart](https://revenuefiles.delaware.gov/docs/gr_rates.pdf) |
| Motor vehicle lessors | 1.9914% | quarterly; none listed | [Division rate chart](https://revenuefiles.delaware.gov/docs/gr_rates.pdf) |
| Petroleum products | Variable; composite rates, up to 2.4218% | Varies | [Division GRT FAQs](https://revenue.delaware.gov/gross-receipts-tax/) |

Exclusions generally start at $100,000 per month and can be as high as $1,250,000, and are provided monthly or quarterly depending on the business activity ([Division GRT FAQs](https://revenue.delaware.gov/gross-receipts-tax/)). A quarterly filer is allowed a deduction of $300,000 per quarter ([30 Del. C. ch. 29](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title30/c029/index.html)). All branches or entities comprising an enterprise with common ownership or common direction and control are allowed only one monthly (or one quarterly) deduction.

**Rates and exclusions vary by specific business activity.** The table shows the most common categories; the Division's rate chart lists every license category with its current rate, return frequency and exclusion. A business conducting multiple activities needs a separate license for each.

## The taxable base

**Gross receipts** means total receipts from goods sold and services rendered in Delaware. No deduction is made on account of the cost of property sold, the cost of materials used, labor costs, interest, discount paid, delivery costs, federal or state taxes or any other expense whatsoever paid or accrued or losses ([30 Del. C. § 2120(a)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title30/c021/index.html); the Division's FAQ lists the same items: cost of goods or property sold, material or labor costs, interest expense, discounts paid, delivery costs, state or federal taxes, or any other expenses allowed).

**How the exclusion works.** The monthly exclusion (deduction) reduces the taxable base before the rate is applied. Example: a general services sole proprietor with $150,000 of gross receipts in a month pays tax on $50,000 ($150,000 − $100,000 exclusion) × 0.3983% = $199.15. Quarterly filers use the quarterly deduction of $300,000 (for a monthly $100,000 category), i.e. three times the monthly amount ([30 Del. C. ch. 29](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title30/c029/index.html)).

**Out-of-state delivery rules.** A retail or wholesale business is not liable for GRT on merchandise shipped out of state; but merchandise picked up or taken delivery of in Delaware is taxable regardless of the ultimate destination. An out-of-state purchaser that picks up in Delaware is covered only if the seller completes Form 373 (Wholesale Exemption Certificate) before or at the sale, keeps the exemption certificates, and notes the exemption on each invoice; otherwise the sale is taxable ([Division GRT FAQs](https://revenue.delaware.gov/gross-receipts-tax/)).

**Multiple business activities.** A sole proprietor performing both retail sales and professional services needs a separate GRT license and filing for each activity, at the respective rate.

**Online/remote sellers.** If you sell tangible goods to Delaware customers from outside Delaware, physical presence in Delaware triggers the GRT; whether economic nexus alone suffices is not settled on the Division's pages — treat it as unconfirmed and refer.

## Deadlines and filing

Returns and payments are due either monthly or quarterly, depending on a business' total gross receipts. The Division of Revenue uses a "look-back period" to determine whether a business is a monthly or quarterly filer ([Division GRT FAQs](https://revenue.delaware.gov/gross-receipts-tax/)):

| Filer | Due date | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly filer | On or before the 20th day of each month, for the immediately preceding month's gross receipts | [30 Del. C. § 2902(b)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title30/c029/index.html); [Division GRT FAQs](https://revenue.delaware.gov/gross-receipts-tax/) |
| Quarterly filer | On or before the last day of the first month following the close of the quarter | [30 Del. C. § 2902(c)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title30/c029/index.html); [Division GRT FAQs](https://revenue.delaware.gov/gross-receipts-tax/) |

All new businesses are automatically set up as quarterly gross receipts tax filers ([Division GRT FAQs](https://revenue.delaware.gov/gross-receipts-tax/)). A month with receipts at or below the exclusion still produces no tax, but the return must still be filed.

Register through the Delaware One Stop business registration system before commencing operations, at the Division of Revenue's [Step 4: gross receipts taxes](https://revenue.delaware.gov/business-tax-forms/doing-business-in-delaware/step-4-gross-receipts-taxes/) page.

## Penalties and interest

Gross receipts tax returns filed late are subject to a penalty of 5% per month, plus interest of 0.5% per month from the original due date until paid. In addition, an additional penalty of 1% per month (not to exceed 25%) is imposed for failure to pay, in whole or in part, the tax liability shown due on a timely filed return ([Division GRT FAQs](https://revenue.delaware.gov/gross-receipts-tax/), citing 30 Del. C. §§ 533, 534). Presenting an invalid exemption certificate makes the seller liable for the tax computed without the exclusion, plus interest and a § 535(a) penalty of 75% of the underpayment attributable to sales covered by the invalid certificate ([30 Del. C. § 2902](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title30/c029/index.html)).

## Cross-border points

- **The GRT follows the seller's Delaware activity, not the customer's location.** Merchandise picked up or delivered in Delaware is taxable even if it leaves the state; merchandise shipped out of state by the seller is not ([Division GRT FAQs](https://revenue.delaware.gov/gross-receipts-tax/)).
- **Out-of-state purchasers** claiming the wholesale exemption must be documented on Form 373 at or before the sale, with certificates retained and invoices marked.
- **GRT is not a pass-through tax** to the customer (unlike a sales tax), though some businesses choose to reflect it in pricing. GRT paid is a deductible business expense on federal Schedule C (and therefore reduces Delaware taxable income).

## Worked examples

**Example 1: general services sole proprietor (hypothetical).** A consultant (general services category) has $150,000 of gross receipts in a month. Taxable base: $150,000 − $100,000 exclusion = $50,000. Tax: $50,000 × 0.3983% = $199.15. The rates and the exclusion are the confirmed figures from the skill's own rates table ([30 Del. C. § 2301(d)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title30/c023/index.html)).

**Example 2: retailer with a below-exclusion month (hypothetical).** A retailer has $80,000 of gross receipts in a month, below the $100,000 exclusion. No tax is due, but the return must still be filed for the period.

## Ask the client first

1. **What is the primary business activity?** It sets the license category, the rate and the exclusion. Straddling categories requires the primary activity to be determined, and separate licenses for each distinct activity.
2. **When did the business start?** New businesses are set up as quarterly filers; the first filing follows the quarterly due date.
3. **Are there multiple activities or locations?** Each activity needs its own license; additional locations add license fees per the rate chart.
4. **Are any sales to out-of-state purchasers picked up in Delaware?** Form 373 exemption certificates and invoice notations must be in place.
5. **What were last period's gross receipts?** The look-back period determines monthly versus quarterly filing.

## Record-keeping and classification

- Maintain monthly gross receipts records by business activity category. The Division may audit and reclassify the business activity if the registered category does not match actual operations.
- On bank statements, payments typically appear as "DE DIV REVENUE GRT", "STATE OF DE BUS TAX" or similar.

## When to refuse or refer

- Petroleum products variable (composite) rates — refer to the Division of Revenue for the current composite rate.
- Insurance premium tax, bank and utility license taxes — separate regimes; refer.
- Nexus analysis for complex multi-state operations — requires legal analysis; refer.
- Audit defense or reclassification disputes — professional representation.
- Wilmington or other municipal net profits taxes — separate municipal taxes outside this skill.
