# MI Corporate Income Tax

## Michigan Corporate Income Tax (CIT)

Michigan imposes a 6% flat Corporate Income Tax on the apportioned business income of C-corporations under the Income Tax Act of 1967 as amended by Public Act 38 of 2011 (MCL 206.601 et seq.). Pass-through entities are not subject to CIT but may elect the 4.25% Flow-Through Entity Tax (FTE) under PA 135 of 2021 as a SALT-cap workaround. The Small Business Alternative Credit reduces a qualifying small C-corp's liability to 1.8% of its adjusted business income (MCL 206.671(4)). Apportionment is single sales factor with market-based sourcing; no throwback. Filing is on Form 4891 with mandatory unitary combined returns. Pre-2012 MBT credits may keep certain taxpayers on the legacy Michigan Business Tax through 2031. Tax year 2025.

## Who this applies to

- **Scope heading** -- 

### 1.1 Who is subject to CIT

- **Taxpayer subject to CIT** -- The Michigan CIT applies to taxpayers that are C-corporations for federal income tax purposes under MCL 206.605(4) and MCL 206.611(1). A "taxpayer" for CIT purposes means a corporation, insurance company, financial institution, or a unitary business group with nexus in Michigan and that is subject to federal taxation as a C-corp.  _(MCL 206.605(4); MCL 206.611(1))_
- **C-corp determination examples** -- A corporation incorporated under state law without an S-election → C-corp → subject to CIT. An LLC that has elected to be taxed as a corporation on Form 8832 and has not made an S-election (Form 2553) → C-corp → subject to CIT. A corporation that has revoked its S-election or had it terminated → C-corp from the date of revocation/termination → subject to CIT for that period.

### 1.2 Who is NOT subject to CIT

- **S-corporations excluded** -- S-corporations (federal Form 1120-S filers) are not subject to CIT. The shareholders report their distributive share on Michigan Form MI-1040 individual returns. The S-corp itself may elect to pay the Flow-Through Entity Tax under PA 135 of 2021.
- **Partnerships and LLCs taxed as partnerships excluded** -- Partnerships and LLCs taxed as partnerships (federal Form 1065 filers) are not subject to CIT. Partners report on their individual Michigan returns. The partnership may elect the FTE.
- **Disregarded single-member LLCs** -- Single-member LLCs disregarded for federal tax purposes: the income is reported on the owner's federal return; if the owner is a C-corp, the C-corp owner is subject to CIT; if the owner is an individual, the income flows to Form MI-1040.
- **Sole proprietorships excluded** -- Sole proprietorships are not subject to CIT.
- **Tax-exempt organizations** -- Tax-exempt organizations under IRC §501(c) are not subject to CIT, except to the extent they have unrelated business taxable income (UBTI) subject to federal Form 990-T, in which case the UBTI is subject to CIT.  _(MCL 206.625)_

### 1.3 Separate tax regimes (out of scope for this skill)

- **Insurance companies separate regime** -- Insurance companies are taxed under MCL 206.635 et seq. at 1.25% of gross direct premiums written on Michigan business (with a retaliatory tax overlay). Form 4905.  _(MCL 206.635 et seq.)_
- **Financial institutions separate regime** -- Financial institutions are taxed under MCL 206.651 et seq. at 0.29% of net capital (a franchise-style tax). Form 4908.  _(MCL 206.651 et seq.)_
- **Bank/thrift/savings/insurance refusal** -- If the taxpayer is a bank, thrift, savings institution, or insurance carrier, refuse to proceed under this skill and refer to a specialist or to the Michigan Department of Treasury financial institution / insurance tax guidance.

### 1.4 Nexus

- **Bright-line nexus gross receipts threshold** -- $350,000 USD (gross receipts sourced to Michigan in the tax year, active solicitation of sales)  _(MCL 206.621(1))_
- **Physical presence nexus** -- A taxpayer has substantial nexus with Michigan if it has a physical presence in the state for any period during the tax year, or if it actively solicits sales in Michigan and has gross receipts of $350,000 or more sourced to Michigan in the tax year (the so-called "$350k bright-line nexus" test enacted in PA 38 of 2011, predating the federal Wayfair economic-nexus framework for sales tax).  _(MCL 206.621(1))_
- **P.L. 86-272 protection** -- Public Law 86-272 (15 U.S.C. §§381-384) continues to protect taxpayers whose Michigan activity is limited to soliciting orders for sales of tangible personal property that are approved and shipped from outside Michigan. P.L. 86-272 does not protect: services (limited to tangible personal property), sales of intangibles, and activities that go beyond solicitation (in-state inventory, installation, post-sale service, etc.).  _(15 U.S.C. §§381-384)_
- **P.L. 86-272 filing requirement and sales factor inclusion** -- Even when P.L. 86-272 protects a taxpayer from CIT on net income, the taxpayer is still required to file a return and the Michigan receipts are still included in the denominator of the sales factor of any unitary affiliate that does have nexus. The Michigan Department of Treasury has not yet formally adopted the Multistate Tax Commission's 2021 revised P.L. 86-272 statement targeting internet activities, so the traditional P.L. 86-272 analysis still controls for Michigan as of tax year 2025.

## Rates, bands and thresholds

- **Rate and tax base heading** -- 

### 2.1 Rate

- **CIT flat rate** -- 6% percent (of the apportioned tax base; flat, no graduated brackets, no surtax; unchanged since PA 38 of 2011 took effect January 1, 2012)  _(MCL 206.623)_

## The taxable base

### 2.2 Starting point: federal taxable income

- **CIT tax base starting point** -- The Michigan CIT tax base starts with the taxpayer's federal taxable income as reported on federal Form 1120, Line 28 (taxable income before NOL deduction and special deductions), subject to a series of additions and subtractions. This is fundamentally different from the pre-2012 Michigan Business Tax, which used a modified gross-receipts base; the post-2011 CIT is a true net-income tax that piggybacks on the federal return.  _(MCL 206.623 to MCL 206.625)_

### 2.3 Additions to the federal base

- **Interest income and dividends from other states' obligations** -- Interest income and dividends from obligations of other states and their political subdivisions (federal exempt under IRC §103) -- added back.  _(MCL 206.623(2))_
- **Taxes on or measured by net income** -- Taxes on or measured by net income, including the CIT itself if deducted federally -- added back.  _(MCL 206.623(2))_
- **Federal NOL deduction addback** -- Federal NOL deduction to the extent claimed on the federal return -- added back (Michigan allows only its own "business loss" deduction).  _(MCL 206.623(2)(c))_
- **Intangible expense addback** -- Royalty, interest, and other expenses paid to a related person that is not included in the same unitary business group (the "intangible expense addback"), unless the related-party recipient is subject to tax in another state and the transaction has a non-tax business purpose.  _(MCL 206.623(2)(g))_
- **Bonus depreciation decoupling** -- Bonus depreciation under IRC §168(k) -- for tax years beginning after December 31, 2024, federal taxable income must be calculated as if IRC §§168(k), 168(n) and 174A were not in effect, so federal bonus depreciation is added back on Form 4891. Michigan does not conform to the One Big Beautiful Bill Act's restoration of 100% bonus depreciation.  _(MCL 206.607(1)(b); Form 4891)_
- **UBT addback** -- Unincorporated Business Tax (UBT) addback -- if the taxpayer is a member of a unitary business group and a flow-through entity in the group paid an unincorporated business or replacement tax in another state, that tax is added back when computing the CIT base of the unitary group.  _(MCL 206.623(2)(h))_
- **FTE addback** -- If a C-corp is the owner of a partnership or S-corp that elected the Michigan FTE, the C-corp owner adds back its share of the FTE tax paid at the entity level, because Michigan then provides a refundable credit to the owner to avoid double taxation.  _(MCL 206.673)_

### 2.4 Subtractions from the federal base

- **Interest income from U.S. obligations** -- Interest income from U.S. obligations (federal bonds, T-bills) -- subtracted (this conforms to the federal preemption under 31 U.S.C. §3124).  _(MCL 206.623(3))_
- **Dividends-received deduction** -- Michigan generally allows a 100% DRD for dividends received from a corporation in the same unitary business group (because they would otherwise be intercompany eliminations) and conforms to the federal DRD for other dividends to the extent of the federal §243 deduction allowed.  _(MCL 206.623(3))_
- **Michigan business loss subtraction** -- The Michigan "business loss" deduction under MCL 206.623(4), computed separately from the federal NOL.  _(MCL 206.623(3))_
- **Flow-through income subtraction** -- Income from a flow-through entity to the extent that income was already taxed via the FTE at the entity level (the C-corp owner subtracts to avoid double taxation, then claims the refundable FTE credit).  _(MCL 206.623(3))_

### 2.5 Federal income tax is NOT deductible

- **No federal income tax deduction** -- Unlike a handful of states (Alabama, Iowa, Louisiana, Missouri until 2022) that have historically allowed a deduction for federal income tax paid, Michigan is fully decoupled from federal income tax as a deduction. Federal income tax expense reflected in the financial statements but not in federal taxable income is not deductible for Michigan CIT.

### 2.6 Michigan business loss (not a federal-style NOL)

- **Federal NOL added back** -- FTI is increased by any federal net operating loss carryback or carryover deduction; Michigan does not follow the federal CARES Act / TCJA framework.  _(MCL 206.623(2)(c))_
- **Michigan "business loss"** -- A business loss incurred in a tax year ending after December 31, 2011 is deducted in the tax year immediately succeeding the loss year, then successively over the next 9 taxable years; there is no carryback and no federal 80% limitation.  _(MCL 206.623(4))_
- **Post-apportionment computation** -- The business loss deduction is taken against Michigan-apportioned income (i.e., apply apportionment first, then deduct the loss).  _(MCL 206.623(4))_
- **§382 conformity** -- NOLs do not survive a §382 ownership change to the extent the federal §382 limitation applies (Michigan conforms to §382).

## 3. Small Business Alternative Credit

- **SBAC heading** -- 

### 3.1 Statutory basis

- **SBAC statutory basis and effect** -- MCL 206.671 provides the Small Business Alternative Credit (SBAC). When the taxpayer qualifies, the credit is the amount by which the CIT exceeds 1.8% of adjusted business income, reducing the net tax to 1.8% of ABI. The taxpayer still files Form 4891 and Form 4893 (the SBAC computation form).  _(MCL 206.671(4))_

### 3.2 The three eligibility tests

- **Gross receipts threshold (business income test)** -- 20,000,000 USD (gross receipts must not exceed this amount for SBAC eligibility)  _(PA 38 of 2011)_
- **Adjusted business income threshold (business income test)** -- Adjusted business income less the loss adjustment at or below $1,300,000 as enacted, adjusted annually for inflation using the Detroit consumer price index; the tax year 2025 threshold is $1,795,300 (binding constraint in practice)  _(MCL 206.671(1))_  _(Form 4946)_
- **Officer/shareholder compensation threshold** -- 180,000 USD (total compensation and director's fees to any individual shareholder owning more than 5% of stock or any individual officer must not exceed this during the tax year)
- **Compensation definition** -- "Compensation" for this purpose includes wages, bonuses, deferred comp paid out, distributions classified as compensation, and director's fees.
- **Allocated income test threshold** -- 180,000 USD (the filer is disqualified if the sum of (A) compensation and directors' fees of a shareholder or officer plus (B) that person's percentage of outstanding ownership multiplied by business income less the loss adjustment exceeds this amount)  _(MCL 206.671(1)(a)(ii))_
- **Indexing** -- Only the adjusted business income threshold is indexed (Detroit consumer price index); the $20,000,000 gross receipts and $180,000 allocated-income thresholds are not.  _(MCL 206.671(1)(a), (1)(c))_

### 3.3 Reduced credit (phase-out)

- **Reduced credit bands** -- With allocated income over $160,000 but under $165,000 the credit is reduced by 20%; $165,000 to under $170,000 by 40%; $170,000 to under $175,000 by 60%; $175,000 to under $180,000 by 80%; at $180,000 or more the filer is ineligible. Form 4893 shows the same bands (80% / 60% / 40% / 20% of the credit).  _(MCL 206.671(1)(b))_  _(Form 4893)_
- **Gross receipts phase-out** -- Above $19,000,000 gross receipts the credit is reduced by a fraction: gross receipts over $19,000,000 divided by $1,000,000 (fully phased out at $20,000,000).  _(MCL 206.671(5))_

In practice, because of the multiplicative effect of the reduction and the loss of the full credit, freelance-developer-scale C-corps either qualify cleanly for the 100% credit (typical) or do not qualify at all.

### 3.4 Disqualifications

- **Unitary group failure disqualification** -- A taxpayer is disqualified from the SBAC regardless of meeting the three tests if it is a member of a unitary business group whose combined members fail any test (the tests are applied at the unitary group level, not the entity level).  _(MCL 206.671(6))_
- **Insurance/financial institution disqualification** -- A taxpayer is disqualified from the SBAC if it is an insurance company or financial institution (those entities have separate small-business relief under their respective regimes).
- **FTE election disqualification** -- A taxpayer is disqualified from the SBAC if it elected the FTE at the entity level (a flow-through that elected FTE is not subject to CIT, so the SBAC is irrelevant; the SBAC has no FTE equivalent).

## 4. Apportionment

- **Apportionment heading** -- 

### 4.1 Single sales factor

- **Michigan sales factor formula** -- Michigan sales factor = Michigan-sourced sales ÷ Sales everywhere  _(MCL 206.661)_
- **Repeal of three-factor formula** -- The pre-2008 three-factor (property + payroll + sales) formula is fully repealed. There is no payroll factor, no property factor, and no weighted formula. This was one of the major design features of PA 38 of 2011 and conforms Michigan to the post-2010 single-sales-factor trend.  _(MCL 206.661)_

### 4.2 Market-based sourcing for services and intangibles

- **Market-based sourcing standard** -- Sales other than sales of tangible personal property are sourced to Michigan if the benefit of the service or use of the intangible is received in Michigan (market-based sourcing). This replaced the older cost-of-performance rule that had applied under SBT and earlier CIT.  _(MCL 206.665)_
- **Software developer/SaaS sourcing examples** -- Selling a license or subscription to a Michigan customer for use by Michigan-based users → Michigan-sourced. Selling to an out-of-state customer whose end-users are nationwide → sourced pro-rata by end-user location (the regulations under R 206.665 allow reasonable apportionment of receipts when the benefit is received in multiple states). Selling consulting services performed remotely to a Michigan customer → Michigan-sourced (benefit received where the client uses the work product).  _(R 206.665)_
- **No detailed market-sourcing regulation** -- Michigan has not yet issued the equivalent of California's CCR §25136-2 detailed market-sourcing regulation, so practitioners rely on the statutory "benefit received" standard and the Department's Revenue Administrative Bulletins (notably RAB 2014-5).  _(RAB 2014-5)_

### 4.3 Sales of tangible personal property

- **Destination rule for TPP sales** -- Sales of tangible personal property are Michigan-sourced if the property is delivered or shipped to a purchaser in Michigan, regardless of the f.o.b. point or other conditions of sale (the destination rule).  _(MCL 206.663)_

### 4.4 Throwback repealed

- **No throwback / no throwout** -- Michigan repealed the throwback rule when PA 38 of 2011 took effect. There is no throwback and no throwout in Michigan CIT. Sales of tangible personal property shipped from a Michigan location to a state where the taxpayer is not taxable (e.g., protected by P.L. 86-272) are not thrown back to Michigan and are treated as "nowhere sales" that fall out of the Michigan numerator while remaining in the denominator. This is highly taxpayer-favorable for Michigan-based exporters.

### 4.5 Sales factor numerator and denominator definitions

- **Denominator definition** -- Total sales of the taxpayer (or the unitary business group) wherever sourced, excluding investment-related sales and certain other excluded items.  _(MCL 206.667)_
- **Numerator definition** -- The portion of those sales sourced to Michigan under the rules above.
- **Exclusion of hedging/treasury/securities receipts** -- Hedging transactions, treasury function receipts, and securities sales are generally excluded from both numerator and denominator, preventing them from distorting the apportionment of a non-financial taxpayer.  _(MCL 206.667)_

## 5. Combined / unitary returns

- **Combined/unitary returns heading** -- 

### 5.1 Mandatory unitary combined filing

- **Mandatory combined reporting** -- Michigan requires mandatory unitary combined reporting. The Michigan CIT is one of the original mandatory-combined-reporting states. There is no consolidated election; the determination is made by the existence of a unitary business group.  _(MCL 206.691)_

### 5.2 Defining the unitary business group

- **Common ownership test** -- One member, directly or indirectly, owns or controls more than 50% of the ownership interests with voting rights (or comparable rights) of the other members. The test is "more than 50%", not "at least 50%" -- exactly 50% does not create a unitary group.  _(MCL 206.611(6))_
- **Unitary relationship test** -- The members have business activities or operations that result in a flow of value between them OR are integrated with, dependent upon, or contribute to each other. The flow-of-value standard derives from the U.S. Supreme Court line of cases (Mobil Oil, Container, Allied-Signal) and includes shared management, centralized purchasing, intercompany financing, and other functional integration.  _(MCL 206.611(6))_
- **Mandatory filing when tests met** -- If both tests are met, the entities must file as a unitary group on a single combined return. The election to file separately is not available.

### 5.3 Water's-edge: no foreign inclusion

- **Water's-edge exclusion / foreign operating entity** -- Michigan's combined return is a water's-edge combined return. Members organized under the laws of a foreign country are excluded from the Michigan unitary group, except a "foreign operating entity": a U.S. corporation that would otherwise be in a unitary group including a Michigan-taxable corporation, has substantial operations outside the United States, and derives at least 80% of its income from active foreign business income (IRC §871(l)(1)(B)(ii)). This is favorable to multinational structures because foreign affiliates do not pull foreign profits into the Michigan tax base.  _(MCL 206.607(3))_

### 5.4 Mechanics of the combined return

- **Single Form 4567 filing** -- The unitary group files one Form 4567 (the combined return; the underlying CIT computation flows through Form 4891).
- **Designated member role** -- The designated member signs the return and is the contact for all CIT matters.
- **Intercompany elimination** -- Intercompany transactions between members are eliminated when computing the combined tax base (so that intercompany sales, interest, royalties, dividends do not artificially inflate the base).
- **Combined sales factor** -- Apportionment is computed using the combined sales factor (Michigan sales of all members divided by everywhere sales of all members, after intercompany elimination).
- **Single rate application** -- The 6% rate is applied once to the combined apportioned base.
- **SBAC tested at group level** -- The SBAC is tested at the unitary group level, not separately for each member.

### 5.5 Joining or leaving the group mid-year

- **Short-period return for mid-year changes** -- When a member joins or leaves the unitary group during the year (e.g., an acquisition or disposition), the entity files a short-period CIT return for the portion of the year it was inside or outside the group. The combined return includes the member's activity for the portion of the year it was part of the unitary group.

## 6. MBT legacy regime

- **MBT legacy regime heading** -- 

### 6.1 The MBT-to-CIT transition

- **MBT description** -- The Michigan Business Tax (MBT) under PA 36 of 2007 was Michigan's main business tax from 2008 through 2011. The MBT was a hybrid that combined a 4.95% business income tax with a 0.8% modified gross receipts tax, plus a 21.99% surtax (which was retroactively repealed).  _(PA 36 of 2007)_
- **MBT to CIT transition** -- PA 38 of 2011 replaced the MBT with the CIT effective January 1, 2012. However, the MBT was not fully repealed. The MBT statute remains on the books because of an unusual transition provision.  _(PA 38 of 2011)_

### 6.2 The MBT certificated credit election

- **Certificated credit election details** -- Under MCL 208.1500 (the MBT statute, still in force for this limited purpose), a taxpayer that holds certain certificated credits issued under MBT -- principally Michigan Economic Growth Authority (MEGA) credits, Brownfield Redevelopment Authority credits, Historic Preservation Tax Credits, Renaissance Zone credits, Film Production credits issued before 2015, Certain anchor and battery credits issued to specific large taxpayers -- may elect to continue filing under the MBT rather than the CIT until the credit certificate expires or is fully used, but no later than tax years beginning before January 1, 2032. The original MCL 208.1500 sunset of 2031 reflected the longest-dated MEGA credit certificates.  _(MCL 208.1500)_

### 6.3 Practical effect

- **MBT election mechanics** -- A taxpayer that makes the MBT election: Files Form 4567 under the MBT regime (the "Continuation of MBT" suite). Pays the MBT 4.95% business income tax + 0.8% modified gross receipts tax in lieu of CIT. Claims its certificated credits against MBT liability. The election is all or nothing for the unitary group -- the entire group must continue on MBT.

For freelance developer-scale taxpayers and the typical small-and-mid C-corp that this skill addresses, the MBT election is not relevant: the certificated credits at issue were almost entirely issued to large incumbent manufacturers, film studios, and major redevelopment projects between 2008-2011. If a client asks about MBT, confirm by checking whether they hold any MEGA, Brownfield, or other certificated credit. If they do not, the answer is simply: the taxpayer files CIT.

### 6.4 Refusal trigger

- **MBT vs CIT trade-off refusal** -- If a taxpayer does hold an MBT certificated credit and is considering whether to remain on MBT or move to CIT, this skill does not provide the trade-off analysis. Refuse and refer to a Michigan-specialist preparer, because the analysis depends on the remaining undrawn credit amount, the projected income trajectory, and the MBT-to-CIT recapture rules.

## 7. Flow-Through Entity Tax (FTE)

- **FTE heading** -- 

### 7.1 Statutory basis: PA 135 of 2021

- **FTE statutory basis** -- PA 135 of 2021, enacted on December 20, 2021 and codified at MCL 206.801-206.847, created the Michigan Flow-Through Entity Tax (FTE). The FTE is an elective entity-level tax on partnerships and S-corporations that allows them to deduct the Michigan income tax at the federal level (avoiding the $10,000 SALT cap on individual itemized deductions) -- the classic "SALT cap workaround" structure that more than 30 states have adopted following IRS Notice 2020-75.  _(MCL 206.801-206.847)_
- **Retroactive election** -- The election was made retroactive to tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2021, allowing taxpayers to claim a federal deduction for 2021 Michigan state income tax that had already been paid at the individual level through estimated payments and withholding. This retroactivity is unusual and was a notable feature of PA 135.  _(PA 135 of 2021)_

### 7.2 Eligible entities

- **Eligible entities for FTE election** -- Partnerships filing federal Form 1065 (general partnerships, limited partnerships, LLPs, LLCs taxed as partnerships). S-corporations filing federal Form 1120-S.
- **Ineligible entities for FTE election** -- Not available to: Single-member LLCs disregarded for federal tax (no entity-level federal partnership return). Sole proprietorships. C-corporations (which are subject to CIT, not FTE). Publicly traded partnerships under §7704.

### 7.3 Rate and base

- **FTE rate** -- 4.25% percent (of the entity's apportioned business income, matching the Michigan individual income tax rate, not the 6% CIT rate)  _(MCL 206.51)_
- **FTE base computation** -- The base is computed at the entity level using market-based single-sales-factor apportionment (parallel to the CIT apportionment rules).
- **Rationale for 4.25% rate** -- The rationale for using the 4.25% individual rate rather than the 6% C-corp rate is that the FTE substitutes for the individual income tax that the partners/shareholders would otherwise pay on their distributive shares.

### 7.4 Election mechanics

- **Filing form for election** -- The election is made by filing Form 5772 (FTE Annual Return) and paying the tax.
- **Election deadline** -- For tax years beginning on and after January 1, 2024, the election must be made on or before the last day of the ninth month after the end of the tax year (September 30 for calendar-year filers); quarterly estimated payments are required.  _(MCL 206.813)_
- **Three-year lock-in** -- The election is irrevocable for three tax years. A 2025 election binds the entity through 2027.  _(MCL 206.813(2))_
- **Entity-level election binds all members** -- The election is made at the entity level -- all members are bound by it. There is no member-level opt-out.

### 7.5 Credit to members

- **Refundable credit mechanics** -- Members (partners, shareholders) of the electing entity receive a refundable credit equal to the member's allocated share of the FTE tax paid by the entity. The member reports the distributive share in income on the Michigan return and then claims the refundable credit, producing the same net Michigan tax result as if the FTE had not been elected -- but with the federal SALT-deduction benefit captured at the entity level.  _(MCL 206.254 (individual members); MCL 206.673 (corporate members))_

### 7.6 Federal deduction mechanics

- **Federal §164(a)(1) deduction** -- For federal purposes, the FTE is deductible by the partnership/S-corp as a business tax under IRC §164(a)(1) (state and local taxes paid in carrying on a trade or business), which is not subject to the §164(b)(6) $10,000 SALT cap on individual itemized deductions. IRS Notice 2020-75 explicitly blessed this structure. The FTE deduction reduces the federal ordinary business income of the entity, reducing the federal taxable income of every owner.  _(IRC §164(a)(1); IRS Notice 2020-75)_

### 7.7 When to elect

- **When FTE is beneficial** -- The FTE election is generally beneficial when: The member is an individual who would otherwise hit the $10,000 SALT cap (almost always true for high-income members). The entity has stable income so that the 3-year lock-in is acceptable. The state Michigan tax savings at the federal level (federal marginal rate × Michigan tax) exceeds the administrative costs of the entity-level filing.
- **When FTE is not beneficial** -- It is generally not beneficial when: Members are tax-exempt or out-of-state non-residents (who would not have hit the SALT cap anyway). The entity expects a loss in any of the next three years (the lock-in becomes burdensome). C-corp members (which can already deduct state tax fully and gain nothing from the workaround, and may face complications with the refundable credit mechanics).

### 7.8 Interaction with the OBBBA SALT cap increase

- **OBBBA SALT cap increase** -- The One Big Beautiful Bill Act of July 4, 2025 (PL 119-21) raised the federal SALT cap for individuals from $10,000 to $40,000 for tax years 2025-2029 with a phase-down for AGI over $500,000. This reduces but does not eliminate the benefit of state pass-through entity taxes. For most high-income Michigan partners/shareholders, the FTE is still beneficial because Michigan income tax (4.25% of substantial flow-through income) plus property tax plus other state taxes still exceeds the new $40,000 cap. The 3-year lock-in is now somewhat less attractive because the federal benefit could change again at the end of 2029.  _(PL 119-21)_

## Deadlines and filing

- **Filing and estimated tax heading** -- 

### 8.1 Annual return

- **Annual return forms** -- The annual CIT return is Form 4891 (Corporate Income Tax Annual Return) for a single corporation, or Form 4567 (the unitary business group return) for a combined group, with each member reported on Form 4897.

### 8.2 Due date

- **Annual return due date** -- The annual return is due on the last day of the 4th month following the close of the tax year. For calendar-year taxpayers, this is April 30.  _(MCL 206.681)_
- **Comparison to federal due date** -- This is one month later than the federal corporate return for calendar-year taxpayers (April 15). Michigan deliberately set the due date one month after the federal deadline to allow the federal return to be completed first.

### 8.3 Extension

- **Extension mechanics** -- A taxpayer may request an automatic 8-month extension by filing Form 4 (Application for Extension of Time to File Michigan Tax Returns) by the original due date and paying the estimated tax. The extension is for time to file only, not time to pay; underpayment of the final liability through estimated payments and the extension payment triggers interest under MCL 205.23.  _(MCL 205.23)_

### 8.4 Estimated tax payments

- **Estimated payment liability threshold** -- 800 USD (a taxpayer that reasonably expects its liability for the tax year to exceed this amount must make quarterly estimated payments)  _(MCL 206.681(2))_

### 8.5 Estimated payment due dates

**Estimated payment due dates for calendar-year taxpayer**  _(MCL 206.681(3))_

| Quarter | Due date |
| --- | --- |
| Q1 | April 15 |
| Q2 | July 15 |
| Q3 | October 15 |
| Q4 | January 15 of the following year |

- **Safe harbor installment rule** -- Each quarterly payment is 25% of the estimated annual liability (or the quarter's estimated base). No interest or penalty is assessed if (a) the sum of the estimated payments equals at least 85% of the liability and each payment reasonably approximates the liability incurred during that quarter, or (b) the preceding year's CIT liability was $20,000 or less and the taxpayer paid 4 equal installments totaling the preceding year's liability.  _(MCL 206.681(3)(a)-(b))_
## Penalties and interest

- **Underpayment interest** -- Underpayment triggers interest under MCL 205.23 at the prevailing Treasury rate. There is no separate underpayment penalty form analogous to federal Form 2220; the interest is assessed by the Department on examination or self-reported on Form 4899.  _(MCL 205.23)_

### 8.6 Estimated tax form

- **Estimated tax filing method** -- Estimated payments are made via Form 4913 (CIT Quarterly Estimated Return) or electronically through Michigan Treasury Online (MTO).
- **Mandatory e-filing** -- Software-prepared CIT returns must be filed electronically; corporations filing 10 or more combined returns must e-file. A CIT filer with Michigan-apportioned or allocated gross receipts under $350,000 does not file an annual return at all.  _(Treasury CIT FAQ; MCL 206.685(1))_

### 9.2 Scope of this skill

- **Local city tax out of scope** -- This skill does not prepare local city income tax returns. If the taxpayer has nexus with any Michigan city that imposes a local income tax, refer to the city-specific filing instructions (Detroit Form 5297 corporate, Grand Rapids Form GR-1120, etc.). Local cities use their own apportionment formulas (most are still three-factor) and have their own due dates (most are April 30 for calendar-year corps).
- **No CIT credit for local city tax** -- The CIT does not provide any credit for local city income tax, so a Detroit-based corporation pays the full 6% state CIT plus the 2% Detroit corporate income tax on Detroit-apportioned income, for an effective combined rate of approximately 8%.

## Cross-border points

- **P.L. 86-272** -- federal protection for solicitation-only sales of tangible personal property (see Nexus above); filing is still required and Michigan receipts still enter the sales factor of nexus-bearing affiliates.
- **No throwback** -- Michigan repealed throwback; unapportioned sales to Michigan in a no-tax jurisdiction stay out of the sales factor numerator.
- **Water's-edge** -- the unitary group excludes foreign operating entities (MCL 206.607(3)); foreign affiliate questions are a refusal trigger.
- **Individual cross-border** -- nonresident and part-year individual situations are handled in the mi-cross-border-tax skill, not here.

## Worked examples

### Worked example 1: single-shareholder C-corp tested against the SBAC

**Facts.** Acme Software Studios, Inc. is a Michigan C-corp incorporated in Michigan. For tax year 2025 it reports:

- Federal taxable income (Form 1120, Line 28): $400,000.
- All sales are to Michigan customers (single-state operation; 100% Michigan-sourced).
- One shareholder, who is also CEO: receives $150,000 W-2 compensation, plus $50,000 of pre-tax 401(k) elective deferrals, for total compensation of $150,000 (the 401(k) deferrals come out of wages for compensation-test purposes -- they are still W-2 compensation under MCL 206.671).
- No other officers or 5%+ shareholders.
- Gross receipts: $1,200,000.

**Step 1 -- Compute the tax base.** Federal taxable income is $400,000. Michigan decouples from federal bonus depreciation for tax years beginning after December 31, 2024 (MCL 206.607(1)(b)), but Acme claimed no federal bonus depreciation, so no addback. No muni bond interest, no related-party expenses, no FTE.

**Step 2 -- Apportion.** Single sales factor: $1,200,000 Michigan ÷ $1,200,000 everywhere = 100%. Apportioned base = $400,000 × 100% = $400,000.

**Step 3 -- CIT before credits.** $400,000 × 6% = **$24,000**.

**Step 4 -- SBAC tests (MCL 206.671(1)).**

- Adjusted business income less loss adjustment: $400,000 ≤ $1,795,300 (2025 indexed threshold) ✓
- Gross receipts $1,200,000 ≤ $20,000,000 ✓
- Compensation test (1)(a)(i): $150,000 ≤ $180,000 ✓
- Allocated income test (1)(a)(ii): compensation $150,000 plus ownership 100% × (business income $400,000 − loss adjustment $0) = $550,000, which exceeds $180,000 → **disqualified**.

**Step 5 -- Credit.** No SBAC (allocated income test failed). Net CIT = **$24,000**. (Had the filer qualified, the credit would have been $24,000 − 1.8% × $400,000 = $16,800, leaving $7,200.)

**Step 6 -- Filing.** File Form 4891 by April 30, 2026. Estimated tax IS required: the expected liability exceeds $800, so quarterly Form 4913 estimates are due April 15, July 15, October 15, and January 15. No FTE -- Acme is a C-corp.

**Outcome.** Acme pays $24,000 Michigan CIT; the SBAC fails on the allocated-income test even though the ABI and gross receipts tests pass.

### Worked example 2: multistate C-corp apportionment and estimates

**Facts.** Velocity Robotics Corp. is a Delaware-incorporated C-corp with operations in Michigan, Ohio, and California. For tax year 2025:

- Federal taxable income (Form 1120, Line 28): $5,000,000.
- Gross receipts: $40,000,000.
- Sales by destination:
  - $10,000,000 of robot hardware shipped to Michigan customers.
  - $8,000,000 of robot hardware shipped to Ohio customers.
  - $12,000,000 of robot hardware shipped to California customers.
  - $6,000,000 of software/SaaS to enterprise customers, of which the benefit is received: $1,500,000 in Michigan, $2,000,000 in California, $2,500,000 elsewhere.
  - $4,000,000 of consulting services delivered remotely; of the engagements, $500,000 to Michigan-based clients, $1,500,000 to California clients, $2,000,000 to clients in Texas, NY, and other states.
- The company has manufacturing in Ohio and sales offices in Michigan. It does not qualify for the SBAC ($5M business income > $1,795,300, the 2025 indexed threshold).
- CEO compensation $800,000; multiple shareholders.

**Step 1 -- Compute Michigan sales numerator.**

- Tangible personal property to Michigan: $10,000,000 (destination rule, MCL 206.663).
- SaaS benefit received in Michigan: $1,500,000.
- Consulting services benefit received in Michigan: $500,000.
- **Total Michigan-sourced sales: $12,000,000.**

Note: Throwback does not apply. Hardware shipped from the Ohio plant to customers in states where Velocity is not taxable (e.g., a small state with no nexus) is not thrown back to Michigan.

**Step 2 -- Sales factor denominator.** Total sales everywhere = $10M + $8M + $12M + $6M + $4M = $40,000,000.

**Step 3 -- Sales factor.** $12,000,000 ÷ $40,000,000 = **30.0%**.

**Step 4 -- Apportioned base.** $5,000,000 × 30.0% = $1,500,000.

**Step 5 -- CIT.** $1,500,000 × 6% = **$90,000**.

**Step 6 -- SBAC.** Disqualified: business income $5M exceeds the $1,795,300 indexed ceiling (2025); CEO comp $800k exceeds the $180k ceiling. No SBAC.

**Step 7 -- Estimated tax.** Liability > $800, so Velocity must make quarterly estimates of at least $22,500 each (≥25% of $90,000), due April 15, July 15, October 15, and January 15.

**Step 8 -- Local city tax.** If Velocity has sales-office nexus with Detroit (i.e., a Detroit office), it also owes Detroit corporate income tax at 2.0% on Detroit-apportioned income -- a separate filing not addressed here.

**Outcome.** Michigan CIT = **$90,000**, plus quarterly estimates and potential Detroit local tax.

### Worked example 3: partnership FTE election

**Facts.** Northern Lights Consulting LLC is a Michigan-organized LLC taxed as a partnership with two equal members:

- Partner A, Michigan resident, individual.
- Partner B, Michigan resident, individual.

For tax year 2025:

- Federal ordinary business income (Form 1065, Line 22): $600,000.
- Apportionment: 100% Michigan (all clients are Michigan).
- Each partner's distributive share: $300,000.
- Each partner already itemizes federally with property tax and state tax exceeding $40,000 (the post-OBBBA SALT cap for 2025).

**Without FTE election (default).**

- Partnership: no entity-level Michigan tax (pass-through, no CIT).
- Each partner: receives Schedule K-1 showing $300,000 ordinary income; reports on Form MI-1040 and pays $300,000 × 4.25% = **$12,750** Michigan PIT each, total **$25,500**.
- Federal deduction: partner's state tax is an itemized deduction subject to the $40,000 SALT cap. With property tax and other state tax already at $40k, the additional $12,750 of Michigan PIT delivers **zero** federal deduction.

**With FTE election.**

- Partnership files Form 5772, elects FTE for 2025-2027 (3-year lock-in).
- Entity-level FTE: $600,000 × 4.25% = **$25,500** paid by the partnership.
- Federal: the $25,500 reduces the partnership's federal ordinary business income to $574,500 (deductible under §164(a)(1) as an entity-level state tax; not subject to the $40k SALT cap because it is at the entity, not individual, level). Each partner's federal distributive share drops to $287,250 (savings of $12,750 each at the federal level).
- Federal tax savings: at a 35% federal marginal rate, each partner saves $12,750 × 35% = **$4,462** in federal tax.
- Michigan: each partner reports their pre-FTE distributive share ($300,000) on Form MI-1040 and claims a refundable credit for their share of FTE paid ($12,750). Net Michigan tax for each partner is the same as without the election: $0 additional (the $12,750 PIT computed at the individual level is offset by the $12,750 refundable credit).
- Total combined federal + Michigan savings from FTE: **$8,924** for the partnership (~$4,462 per partner).

**Conclusion.** The FTE election saves the partners approximately $8,900 in federal tax for 2025, with no offsetting Michigan cost. The partnership commits to the FTE for 2025-2027. For a 2025 tax year the election must be made on or before September 30, 2026 (the last day of the ninth month after the end of the tax year), and quarterly estimated FTE payments are made via Form 5774.

**Notes:**

- If either partner had been a non-resident of Michigan, the analysis would still favor FTE (Michigan still allows the refundable credit to non-resident members), but the partner's state-of-residence may not give a credit for FTE paid (the credit-mechanism question depends on the home state's PTE-credit policy).
- If the LLC had elected to be taxed as a C-corp federally instead, it would owe 6% CIT (not 4.25% FTE) and would not qualify for the FTE workaround -- though the C-corp itself can deduct state tax federally without SALT cap.

## Ask the client first

- Entity type and federal filing status (C-corp vs pass-through; whether an FTE election was made or is available).
- Whether the federal return claimed bonus depreciation under IRC 168(k)/168(n) or section 174A amortization -- Michigan requires the Form 4891 separation adjustments.
- Whether the corporation is part of a unitary business group (common ownership plus unity of operation) and whether any member is a foreign operating entity.
- Shareholder compensation and ownership percentages, and adjusted business income versus the indexed SBAC threshold, before quoting SBAC eligibility.
- Whether the business has nexus with Detroit or another local income-tax city (separate returns, separate deadlines).
