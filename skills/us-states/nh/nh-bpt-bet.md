# NH Bpt Bet

## New Hampshire Business Profits Tax (BPT) and Business Enterprise Tax (BET)

New Hampshire has no general personal income tax and no general sales tax, but it taxes business activity through two parallel filings: the Business Profits Tax (BPT) at 7.5% on taxable business profits (tax periods ending on or after December 31, 2023) and the Business Enterprise Tax (BET) at 0.55% on the enterprise value tax base of compensation, interest, and dividends paid (tax periods ending on or after December 31, 2022). BET paid is creditable against BPT for the same period, and unused BET credit carries forward 10 years for periods ending on or after December 31, 2014. Both taxes apply to sole proprietorships, partnerships, LLCs, and corporations conducting business activity in NH. The Interest & Dividends Tax was repealed for tax periods beginning on or after January 1, 2025. For taxable periods beginning on or after January 1, 2025, the BPT filing threshold is gross business income of more than $109,000 and the BET filing threshold is gross receipts or an enterprise value tax base of more than $298,000. Tax year 2025.

## Who this applies to

New Hampshire imposes no tax on wages and no general retail sales tax. There is no state individual income tax return and no state sales-tax permit for in-state retail sales of tangible personal property.

What New Hampshire does tax, and what this skill covers:

1. **Business Profits Tax (BPT)** under RSA 77-A (enacted 1970) - a net income tax imposed on every business organization organized for gain or profit carrying on business activity within New Hampshire, including sole proprietorships, partnerships, LLCs (regardless of federal classification), corporations, and S-corporations. The BPT is imposed at the entity level even on pass-through entities - there is no flow-through to owners for NH purposes. A Schedule C filer with NH-source business income files a NH BPT return in the proprietorship's own right.  _(RSA 77-A; NH DRA Business Taxes overview)_

2. **Business Enterprise Tax (BET)** under RSA 77-E (enacted 1993) - a tax on the "enterprise value tax base," the sum of all compensation paid or accrued, interest paid or accrued, and dividends paid by the business enterprise, before special adjustments and apportionment. The BET captures economic activity even when the entity has zero or negative profits. BET paid for a tax period is creditable against the BPT for the same period; unused credit carries forward 10 years for taxable periods ending on or after December 31, 2014 (5 years for earlier periods).  _(RSA 77-E:1; RSA 77-A:5, X; NH DRA BET FAQ)_

### 1.1 Out of scope (refer out)

- **Real Estate Transfer Tax (RETT)** under RSA 78-B - the rate of the tax is $.75 per $100, or fractional part thereof, of the price or consideration for the sale, grant, or transfer; both the grantor and the grantee are required to buy and attach indicia of tax paid. Refer to a real-estate-tax-focused engagement.  _(RSA 78-B:1; RSA 78-B:4)_
- **Meals and Rooms (Rentals) Tax** under RSA 78-A - 8.5 percent of the charge for meals and other prepared food and for lodging. Refer to a hospitality-vertical engagement.  _(RSA 78-A:6)_
- **Communications Services Tax** under RSA 82-A - 7 percent of the gross charge for two-way communications services.  _(RSA 82-A:3)_
- **Property tax** - administered by municipalities, not the NH Department of Revenue Administration.
- **Interest & Dividends Tax (I&D)** under RSA 77 - **REPEALED** for tax periods beginning on or after January 1, 2025. See the section on the repeal below.

The administering agency for BPT and BET is the New Hampshire Department of Revenue Administration (NH DRA), Concord, NH. Payments are made through the DRA's Granite Tax Connect portal.  _(NH DRA BPT FAQ; NH DRA BET FAQ)_

### 2.4 BET filing thresholds

The Commissioner adjusts the BET filing thresholds biennially for inflation, rounding to the nearest $1,000.  _(NH DRA BET FAQ)_

| Taxable periods | Threshold |
| --- | --- |
| Beginning on or after 1/1/2025 | Gross receipts in excess of $298,000 or enterprise value tax base greater than $298,000 |
| Beginning 1/1/2023 through 12/31/2024 | $281,000 (either measure) |
| Ending on or after 12/31/2022 | $250,000 (either measure) |

The two measures are an OR test: exceeding either gross receipts or the enterprise value tax base triggers the filing requirement.  _(NH DRA Business Taxes overview)_

### 2.5 Estimated-payment requirement thresholds

- BPT: four estimated payments are required, at 25% each on the 15th day of the 4th, 6th, 9th, and 12th month of the taxable period, for any tax liability greater than $200.  _(RSA 77-A:6; NH DRA BPT FAQ)_
- BET: for taxable periods ending on or after December 31, 2013, estimates are required if the estimated tax liability exceeds $260 (the same four 25% installments). For periods ending before December 31, 2013 the threshold was $200.  _(NH DRA BET FAQ)_

The thresholds are per tax, not a combined BPT plus BET figure.

## The taxable base

### 3.1 BPT starting point

A business organization applies the provisions of the US Internal Revenue Code, as defined in RSA 77-A:1, XX, consistent with RSA 77-A, in determining gross business profits - before net operating loss and special deductions - with the NH adjustments in Section 3.2.  _(RSA 77-A:3-b)_

The IRC reference date in RSA 77-A:1, XX is December 31, 2018.  _(RSA 77-A:1)_

For multi-state businesses, income is apportioned using a weighted sales factor of two plus the payroll and property factors for periods ending before December 31, 2022, and the sales factor only for periods ending on or after December 31, 2022.  _(NH DRA BPT FAQ; NH DRA Business Taxes overview)_

### 3.2 IRC adjustments (RSA 77-A:3-b) and §179 cap

- The IRC is applied **without section 168(k)** - NH does not conform to federal bonus depreciation.  _(RSA 77-A:3-b, I)_
- The IRC is applied **without section 181**.  _(RSA 77-A:3-b, III)_
- IRC section 179 is applied as provided in RSA 77-A:3-a: for property placed in service on or after January 1, 2018, expense deductions may not exceed $500,000. The higher federal §179 dollar limits in effect for 2025 do not apply to NH because the IRC reference date in RSA 77-A:1, XX is December 31, 2018.  _(RSA 77-A:3-a; RSA 77-A:3-b, IV; RSA 77-A:1, XX)_

### 3.3 Deductions from gross business profits (RSA 77-A:4)

- A deduction of gross business profits attributable to income derived from interest on notes, bonds or other securities of the United States.  _(RSA 77-A:4, II)_
- The reasonable compensation deduction for proprietors, partners, and members (Section 3.4).  _(RSA 77-A:4, III)_
- The NH net operating loss carryover (Section 3.5).  _(RSA 77-A:4, XIII)_

### 3.4 Reasonable compensation deduction (RSA 77-A:4, III)

- **Who qualifies** - In the case of a proprietorship, partnership, or LLC filing as a proprietorship or partnership, a deduction equal to a fair and reasonable compensation for the actual personal services of a natural person who is a proprietor, partner, or member. It is not available to shareholder-employees of C-corporations or S-corporations.  _(RSA 77-A:4, III(a))_
- **Ceiling** - The deduction may not reduce the business organization's taxable business profits to less than zero, and the amounts deducted may not exceed the amount reported as earned income on the federal income tax returns of the proprietor, partner, or member.  _(RSA 77-A:4, III(a))_
- **Record-keeping safe harbor** - In lieu of substantiating the value of personal services, a business organization or group of related business organizations may elect to deduct up to $75,000 as **total compensation for the tax year** (per organization, not per member). Claims above $75,000 require substantiation of the value of the personal services.  _(RSA 77-A:4, III(c))_
- **Interaction with self-employment** - The deduction is a NH BPT-side modification only; it does not change federal Schedule C net profit or federal self-employment tax.  _(RSA 77-A:4, III)_

### 3.5 NH net operating loss (RSA 77-A:4, XIII)

- NH NOL is computed under IRC section 172 as defined in RSA 77-A:1, apportioned in the year incurred under RSA 77-A:3. For tax periods ending on or after December 31, 2022, IRC section 172 in effect per RSA 77-A:1 (the December 31, 2018 version, post-TCJA) is followed; for periods ending before that date but after July 1, 2005, IRC section 172 in effect on December 31, 1996 is followed. The NH-specific 10-year carryforward and generation caps in RSA 77-A:4, XIII apply on top of the federal computation.  _(RSA 77-A:4, XIII; NH DRA BPT FAQ; RSA 77-A:1, XX)_
- **Carryforward** - An NOL may only be carried forward for the 10 years following the loss year. SB 435-FN, which made the 10-year carryforward, took effect July 1, 2022 and applies to tax years ending on or after December 31, 2022.  _(RSA 77-A:4, XIII; NH Senate Calendar No. 7, February 10, 2022)_
- **Annual generation caps** - The NOL generated in a tax year that may be carried forward is capped: $10,000,000 for periods beginning on or after 1/1/2013; $1,000,000 for 7/1/2005 through 12/31/2012; $750,000 for 7/1/2004 through 6/30/2005; $500,000 for 7/1/2003 through 6/30/2004; $250,000 on or before 6/30/2003.  _(RSA 77-A:4, XIII; NH DRA BPT FAQ)_
- **No carryback** for periods ending on or after July 1, 2005; the carryover is limited to losses incurred on or after July 1, 1997.  _(RSA 77-A:4, XIII; NH DRA BPT FAQ)_
- The DP-132 (or DP-132-WE for combined groups) NOL deduction worksheet is required in the year the NOL deduction is used.  _(NH DRA BPT FAQ; NH DRA forms page)_

### 3.6 S-corporations

S-corporations are treated the same as C-corporations for BPT purposes; flow-through items on federal Schedule K are incorporated into corporate income when determining taxable business profits.  _(NH DRA BPT FAQ)_

### 3.7 Combined returns and water's edge

- A "unitary business" means one or more related business organizations engaged in business activity both within and without this state among which there exists a unity of ownership, operation, and use; or an interdependence in their functions.  _(RSA 77-A:1, XIV)_
- A "combined group" means business organizations whose unitary business is conducted within and without New Hampshire through the use of more than one legal entity (Rev 301.07); combined reporting means reporting the group's taxable business profits on a single return (Rev 301.08). There is no separate 50-percent common-ownership test in the NH statute or rules.  _(NH Administrative Rules Rev 300; NH DRA BPT FAQ)_
- Members of a water's edge combined group must file a combined return reporting the group's combined net income. The water's edge group is the unitary group excluding overseas business organizations, with an 80/20 business organization excluded only if the statutory criteria are met.  _(RSA 77-A:6, IV; RSA 77-A:1, XV)_

### 3.8 BPT apportionment and sourcing of receipts

- Single sales factor for taxable periods ending on or after December 31, 2022; before that, a weighted sales factor of two plus payroll and property factors.  _(NH DRA BPT FAQ; NH DRA Business Taxes overview)_
- Under Rev 304.04 (sales factor for periods ending on or after December 31, 2021), the NH numerator includes: sales of tangible personal property delivered in NH; sales of tangible personal property **originating in New Hampshire to a purchaser in another state in which the business organization is not taxable** (a throwback of so-called nowhere sales); sales of services delivered to a location in NH; rents and royalties from NH property or use; intangibles to the extent used in NH; interest where the debtor or encumbered property is in NH; and dividends where the commercial domicile is in NH.  _(NH Administrative Rules Rev 304.04)_
- The pre-2021 sales factor rules (for taxable periods ending before December 31, 2021) are reprinted in Appendix B to Rev 304.04; Rev 304.01 lists activities creating nexus.  _(NH Administrative Rules Rev 300; NH DRA BPT FAQ)_

### 3.9 BET enterprise value tax base (EVTB)

- "Enterprise value tax base" means the sum of all compensation paid or accrued, interest paid or accrued, and dividends paid by the business enterprise, before special adjustments under RSA 77-E:3 or apportionment under RSA 77-E:4.  _(RSA 77-E:1, IX)_
- **Compensation element** - includes wages, and any net earnings from self-employment subject to tax under IRC section 1401, to the extent not included in a deduction taken under RSA 77-A:4, III. NH law and Rev 2402.01 contain **no cap at the federal FICA wage base** for self-employment compensation - the full net SE earnings enter the base.  _(RSA 77-E:1, V(c); NH Administrative Rules Rev 2402.01)_
- **Guaranteed payments to partners are included** in the BET compensation base (the federal Form 1065 deduction of guaranteed payments does not remove them from the BET base).  _(NH DRA BET FAQ; Rev 2402.01)_
- **Intercompany dividends** - the affiliate and the parent can deduct the dividend where both are members of the same affiliated group.  _(NH DRA BET FAQ; RSA 77-E:3)_
- Bank interest paid to depositors is included in a bank's BET base. Non-profits must file the BET return if they meet the filing requirements and are not organizations exempt under IRC section 501(c)(3).  _(NH DRA BET FAQ)_

### 3.10 BET credit against BPT and carryforward

- Taxes paid pursuant to RSA 77-E (BET) are allowed as a credit against the BPT, applied in accordance with RSA 77-E:13.  _(RSA 77-A:5, X)_
- Unused BET credit from taxable periods ending before December 31, 2014 carries forward 5 years; unused credit from taxable periods ending on or after December 31, 2014 carries forward 10 years from the period in which it was paid.  _(NH DRA BET FAQ)_
- The BET-CW credit worksheet and DP-160 schedule of BPT and BET credits are the DRA forms for the computation.  _(NH DRA forms page)_

## Deadlines and filing

### 4.1 Returns and due dates

- Partnership returns (BPT and BET) are due the 15th day of the 3rd month following the end of the taxable period. Corporate, proprietorship, fiduciary, and combined returns are due the 15th day of the 4th month. For calendar-year filers: March 15 (partnerships) and April 15 (all others).  _(RSA 77-A:6, I; NH DRA BPT FAQ; NH DRA BET FAQ)_
- All federal schedules must be attached to original and amended BPT returns; proprietorships must include Schedules C, D, E, F, 4797, and 6252 as applicable.  _(NH DRA BPT FAQ)_
- All taxpayers file the BT-Summary form to report name and address information and summarize taxes due; if the BET return is not required, NO must be checked in Step 2 of the BT-Summary.  _(NH DRA BET FAQ)_

### 4.3 Estimated payments

Four installments of 25% each of the estimated tax, due on the 15th day of the 4th, 6th, 9th, and 12th month of the taxable period, when the liability exceeds the Section 2.5 thresholds. A declaration of estimated tax need not be filed if the estimated tax is less than $200, and a declaration must be filed at the end of any quarter in which estimated tax thereafter exceeds $200.  _(RSA 77-A:6; NH DRA BPT FAQ; NH DRA BET FAQ)_

### 4.4 Extensions

BT-EXT is the DRA's payment form and application for extension of time to file for business taxes.  _(NH DRA forms page)_

## Penalties and interest

- Underpayment of estimated tax accrues an addition computed at the underpayment rate determined under RSA 21-J:28, II for the period of the underpayment.  _(RSA 21-J:32, I)_
- Exceptions to the estimated-tax penalty (filed on DP-2210/2220) include: the installment equals 90 percent of the tax for the taxable period; the amount computed on the prior year's facts and law; or an annualized-income computation. Each quarter stands on its own - the same exception need not be used in all four quarters. A first-year taxpayer with liability over $200 may qualify for an exception under RSA 21-J:32, IV(d).  _(RSA 21-J:32, IV; NH DRA BPT FAQ)_
- Rental income of a married couple cannot be split between spouses unless two separate and distinct proprietorships exist (Rev 307.04(e)).  _(NH DRA BPT FAQ)_

## Cross-border points

- **Federal deductibility of BPT and BET** - BPT and BET are business taxes paid at the entity level; a sole proprietor deducts them on federal Schedule C as taxes and licenses, and entities deduct them as a business expense. They are not pass-through itemized deductions subject to the individual state-and-local tax deduction cap.  _(NH DRA BPT FAQ (returns and federal schedules); general federal treatment)_
- **No PTET workaround needed** - Because the BPT is imposed at the entity level, NH needs no separate pass-through entity tax election.
- **No coordination with federal QBI or SE tax** - The reasonable compensation deduction and NH modifications are NH-side only; federal Schedule SE and QBI are computed independently.
- **Combined groups** - the sales factor numerator is computed for the combined group, including NH-origin sales into states where the member is not taxable.  _(Rev 304.04)_
- **I&D Tax repeal** - The Interest and Dividends Tax (previously 3% on interest and dividend income over $2,400 single / $4,800 joint, with exemptions for residents 65 or older, blind, or disabled) was repealed by House Bill 2 of the 2023 session for tax periods beginning on or after January 1, 2025. Taxable periods beginning on or before December 31, 2024 remain subject to audit and collection. The repeal does NOT reduce BPT or BET liability - a client who says "NH repealed the income tax, so I owe nothing" likely still owes BPT and/or BET.  _(NH DRA news release, January 23, 2025)_

## Worked examples

### Worked example 1: Sole proprietor reasonable-compensation mechanic

A sole proprietor with $200,000 of federal Schedule C net profit, no other NH modifications, and 100% NH apportionment. Claiming the $75,000 safe-harbor reasonable compensation deduction:

```oa-check
name: RCD at safe harbor
inputs:
  profit: 200000
  rcd: 75000
  rate: 0.075
steps:
  - base: profit - rcd
  - bpt: base * rate
expected:
  base: 125000
  bpt: 9375
```

If the proprietor instead substantiates $150,000 of reasonable compensation (above the safe harbor, documented): base $50,000, BPT $3,750.

### Worked example 2: Freelance developer, market-based sourcing

A NH-resident freelance software developer with one corporate client in California and one in NH: $120,000 from the CA client (service delivered to a location in CA - not NH sales) and $40,000 from the NH client (NH sales).

```oa-check
name: Sales factor and BPT
inputs:
  ca_sales: 120000
  nh_sales: 40000
  profit_after_rcd: 80000
  rate: 0.075
steps:
  - everywhere: ca_sales + nh_sales
  - factor: nh_sales / everywhere
  - apportioned: profit_after_rcd * factor
  - bpt: apportioned * rate
expected:
  everywhere: 160000
  factor: 0.25
  apportioned: 20000
  bpt: 1500
```

Market-based sourcing with the throwback limited to NH-origin tangible personal property can apportion a large share of a service business's income out of NH.

### Worked example 3: EVTB computation, NH LLC taxed as partnership

During 2025 the LLC pays $180,000 in wages to two employees, $40,000 in self-employment compensation to one managing member (uncapped - no FICA wage-base limit applies to the NH base), $8,000 of interest on a business line of credit, and no dividends.

```oa-check
name: EVTB and BET
inputs:
  wages: 180000
  se_comp: 40000
  interest: 8000
  rate: 0.0055
steps:
  - evtb: wages + se_comp + interest
  - bet: evtb * rate
expected:
  evtb: 228000
  bet: 1254
```

### Worked example 4: Sole proprietor freelancer, $300k revenue

Aisha, a NH-resident freelance UX designer (sole proprietor, no employees, no business interest). 2025: gross receipts $300,000 ($90,000 NH clients, $210,000 out-of-state clients, services delivered to those clients' locations); business expenses $80,000; federal Schedule C net profit $220,000.

BPT: she substantiates $115,000 of reasonable compensation (above the $75,000 safe harbor, documented with time records and comparable-wage evidence); NH gross business profits after RCD $105,000; NH sales factor 30%; apportioned base $31,500.

BET: her self-employment compensation enters the EVTB uncapped - net SE earnings are $220,000 x 92.35% = $203,170 (federal Schedule SE computation). The federal maximum taxable earnings figure ($176,100 for 2025) is a Social Security cap and does **not** cap the NH BET base. EVTB $203,170; threshold check: EVTB is below $298,000 but gross receipts $300,000 exceed $298,000, so filing is required (OR test). Payroll apportionment 100% NH (services performed from her NH home office).

```oa-check
name: Aisha BPT and BET
inputs:
  profit: 220000
  rcd: 115000
  nh_sales: 90000
  all_sales: 300000
  bpt_rate: 0.075
  se_pct: 0.9235
  bet_rate: 0.0055
steps:
  - gbpb: profit - rcd
  - factor: nh_sales / all_sales
  - apportioned: gbpb * factor
  - bpt: round(apportioned * bpt_rate, 2)
  - se_comp: round(profit * se_pct, 2)
  - bet: round(se_comp * bet_rate, 2)
  - bpt_due: round(bpt - bet, 2)
  - total: bpt
expected:
  gbpb: 105000
  factor: 0.3
  apportioned: 31500
  bpt: 2362.50
  se_comp: 203170
  bet: 1117.44
  bpt_due: 1245.06
  total: 2362.50
```

BPT before credit $2,362.50; BET $1,117.44 fully credited against BPT; BPT due $1,245.06; BET due $1,117.44; total NH tax $2,362.50. On the federal return she deducts the taxes actually paid - $1,245.06 BPT plus $1,117.44 BET = $2,362.50 - on Schedule C as taxes and licenses (cash basis).

### Worked example 5: Multi-employee LLC, guaranteed payments and the safe-harbor ceiling

Granite Marketing LLC (NH LLC taxed as partnership), two member-managers, six employees. 2025: gross receipts $1,800,000 (all NH-sourced); W-2 wages $620,000; member guaranteed payments $180,000 total; other expenses $920,000; interest on a line of credit $25,000; federal Form 1065 ordinary business income (after guaranteed payments deducted federally) $55,000.

BPT: guaranteed payments deducted federally are added back so the partnership starts from full pre-guaranteed-payment profit, then deducts reasonable compensation. The two members claim $150,000 total ($75,000 each) - note the $75,000 record-keeping safe harbor is **total compensation per business organization per tax year**, so a $150,000 claim exceeds the safe harbor and must be substantiated under IRC section 162(a)(1) principles.

BET: guaranteed payments are in the compensation base whether or not deducted federally.

```oa-check
name: Granite Marketing BPT and BET
inputs:
  income_1065: 55000
  gp_addback: 180000
  rcd_total: 150000
  bpt_rate: 0.075
  wages: 620000
  interest: 25000
  bet_rate: 0.0055
steps:
  - gbp: income_1065 + gp_addback
  - base: gbp - rcd_total
  - bpt: base * bpt_rate
  - evtb: wages + gp_addback + interest
  - bet: evtb * bet_rate
  - bpt_due: bpt - bet
expected:
  gbp: 235000
  base: 85000
  bpt: 6375
  evtb: 825000
  bet: 4537.50
  bpt_due: 1837.50
```

BPT $6,375; BET $4,537.50 fully credited; BPT due $1,837.50; total NH tax $6,375. The RCD reduces BPT but does NOT reduce BET - compensation is in the EVTB whether characterized as a partner draw, a guaranteed payment, or W-2 wages.

### Worked example 6: Capital-light C-corporation

Lakeside Software Inc., NH C-corporation. 2025: gross receipts $850,000 (75% NH market); W-2 wages $260,000; no interest; no dividends; federal Form 1120 taxable income $400,000. No reasonable compensation deduction (shareholder-employees already take W-2 wages).

```oa-check
name: Lakeside Software
inputs:
  ti: 400000
  nh_factor: 0.75
  bpt_rate: 0.075
  comp: 260000
  bet_rate: 0.0055
steps:
  - apportioned: ti * nh_factor
  - bpt: apportioned * bpt_rate
  - bet: comp * bet_rate
  - bpt_due: bpt - bet
expected:
  apportioned: 300000
  bpt: 22500
  bet: 1430
  bpt_due: 21070
```

BPT $22,500 dominates; BET $1,430 fully credited; total NH tax $22,500.

### Worked example 7: Loss year - BET still owed, credit carries forward 10 years

Same Granite Marketing LLC, bad year: gross receipts $1,200,000; W-2 wages $580,000; member guaranteed payments $150,000; interest $20,000; federal Form 1065 ordinary business income negative $250,000.

BPT: gross business profits before RCD are negative $100,000 after the guaranteed-payment add-back; the RCD cannot increase the loss, so RCD is $0; an NH NOL of $100,000 is generated (10-year carryforward, subject to the generation caps).

```oa-check
name: Loss year BET
inputs:
  loss: 250000
  gp_addback: 150000
  wages: 580000
  interest: 20000
  bet_rate: 0.0055
steps:
  - gbp: gp_addback - loss
  - nol: loss - gp_addback
  - evtb: wages + gp_addback + interest
  - bet: evtb * bet_rate
expected:
  gbp: -100000
  nol: 100000
  evtb: 750000
  bet: 4125
```

BET $4,125 is owed (thresholds exceeded) and carries forward as a credit against BPT for the next 10 taxable periods.

## Ask the client first

1. Entity type and federal classification - NH taxes the entity, not the owner: sole proprietors file NH-1040, partnerships NH-1065, corporations and S-corporations NH-1120.
2. Gross business income and gross receipts for the year - test the $109,000 BPT and $298,000 BET thresholds (periods beginning on or after 1/1/2025) separately.
3. Compensation paid or accrued - W-2 wages, guaranteed payments, and the proprietor's or partners' net self-employment earnings (uncapped) - plus interest paid and dividends paid, for the BET base.
4. Whether the proprietor, partners, or members render personal services and what reasonable compensation is supportable; whether the $75,000 total safe harbor is being used or exceeded.
5. Where services are delivered and where property is used - the single sales factor and market-based sourcing drive the NH percentage.
6. Whether related entities conduct a unitary business through more than one legal entity (combined return).
7. Prior-year NOLs and unused BET credit (10-year carryforwards) sitting on the books.
